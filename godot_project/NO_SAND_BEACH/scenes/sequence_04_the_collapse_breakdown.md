# NO SAND BEACH — Sequence 4: The Collapse
## Beat-by-Beat Breakdown

**Sequence ID:** `nsb_seq_04_the_collapse`
**Target Duration:** 1:46 (106 seconds)
**Theme:** Reckoning, forgiveness, the cost of becoming a stranger to yourself
**Tone:** Quiet devastation, then something painful opening — not resolution yet, just the first cracks of honesty

**Style:** Fully contemporary throughout. No M2S period treatment — the film is now in Pure's present. Pure in his age-27 design. Delia and Marcus in their age-locked generic designs.

---

## Scene 4A: The Fall (0:00–0:10)

### Environment
- **Location:** Home workspace, night (same desk from Sequence 3's opening beat — the visual callback is intentional)
- **Time:** Night, continuous from Sequence 3's closing

### Characters Present
- Pure (age 27 design, contemporary)

### Beat Sequence

#### Beat 4.01: THE BODY GIVES OUT
- **Type:** Action
- **Actor:** Pure
- **Action:**
  - Pure at the desk, mid-code — his screen still glowing, the work still in front of him
  - He stops. His hand stills on the keyboard. Then his body simply gives — he slides from the chair, slow, boneless, to the floor
  - The camera holds on the screen still running, Pure's chair empty, the desk light still on
- **Duration:** 10 seconds
- **Dialogue:** None — silent fall
- **Camera:** Static medium on desk/screen → Pure slides out of frame → hold on the empty chair and glowing screen
- **Visual Notes:**
  - The collapse is quiet, not dramatic — no crash, no convulsion; the body just stops cooperating
  - The glowing screen is the last visual from Sequence 3's closing beat, now reframed as abandonment: the thing he built has taken everything it could take
  - Hold the empty chair longer than feels comfortable — that's the image
- **Audio Notes:** Faint keyboard click as his hand drags across it going down, then silence. The hum of the laptop. Nothing else.
- **Next Beat:** 4.02 (hospital)
- **Failure Path:** None

## Scene 4B: The Hospital (0:10–1:01)

### Environment
- **Location:** Hospital room — a bed, a chair beside it, a window with diffuse white light, equipment as minimal shapes
- **Time:** Day; Pure is conscious; time of day is deliberately uncertain — could be the next morning, could be three days later
- **Atmosphere:** Quiet, held, the stillness of a room where hard things are about to be said

### Characters Present
- Pure (age 27 design, lying in hospital bed)
- Delia (age-locked generic design)
- Marcus (age-locked generic design — enters midway through)

### Beat Sequence

#### Beat 4.02: THE HAND
- **Type:** Establishing / Emotional
- **Actor:** Delia, Pure
- **Action:**
  - Hospital room. Pure in the bed, staring at the ceiling. Delia seated beside him, her hand over his — not clutching, just resting there
  - She's not crying. Her face holds something harder than tears: a woman who has seen catastrophe before and has not yet decided how to carry this one
  - Pure is awake. He hasn't looked at her yet.
- **Duration:** 8 seconds
- **Dialogue:** None yet — the scene opens in silence
- **Camera:** Wide hospital room (sparse, minimal detail) → push slowly to the two hands, one over the other → hold
- **Visual Notes:**
  - The hands are the visual anchor of this entire scene — return to them at least once more before the scene ends
  - Delia should read tired in a way that is different from her Sequence 1 tired: not the weariness of a night shift but the weariness of something finally arriving
- **Audio Notes:** Hospital ambient — distant footsteps in a hall, a monitor beep that isn't dramatic, just present. Then quiet.
- **Next Beat:** 4.03 (Delia speaks)
- **Failure Path:** None

---

#### Beat 4.03: DELIA SPEAKS
- **Type:** Dialogue (the film's most important speech)
- **Actor:** Delia (primary), Pure (listening)
- **Action:**
  - Delia begins to speak — not a prepared speech, something being worked out in real time, each sentence arriving like it costs something
  - Pure finally turns his head to look at her — the first time we see him look at her in this scene
  - She doesn't flinch. She holds his eyes the whole time.
- **Duration:** 20 seconds
- **Dialogue:**
  - Delia: "I worked three jobs because I didn't want you to think you had to be rich to be loved."
  - *(pause — she finds it)*
  - Delia: "But I made you think you had to be perfect to be worthy. That's on me."
  - *(harder now)*
  - Delia: "I saved your body. I didn't save your heart. That's on you."
- **Camera:** Close on Delia's face for the speech — hold, no cuts — let the camera find Pure's face only for the last line
- **Visual Notes:**
  - This speech is the emotional center of the film. Do not rush it, do not cut away during it. The camera stays on Delia until the last line and then — only then — finds Pure's face for "That's on you."
  - Her expression is not anger. It is not warmth either. It is something beyond both: a mother who loves her son enough to tell him the true thing.
  - Pure's face when the camera finds him: not broken, something more complicated — like a room filling with light from under a door
- **Audio Notes:** Her voice is steady. If it wavers, it's once, briefly, at "save your heart" — then she recovers. The silences between her sentences are as important as the sentences.
- **Next Beat:** 4.04 (Marcus arrives)
- **Failure Path:** None

---

#### Beat 4.04: THE TWENTY YEARS
- **Type:** Dialogue (confrontation — but the right kind)
- **Actor:** Marcus, Pure; Delia present but no longer speaking
- **Action:**
  - Marcus enters the room, stands at the foot of the bed — not hostile, but not easy either
  - The look between the two brothers: everything unsaid in twenty years, right there
  - This is not a fight. It's the conversation after every fight has run out of road.
  - Brief, direct: Marcus names the resentment ("I did everything right. I'm still struggling. You get all of her, and you're still the one she calls first."), Pure names it back ("I didn't want her watching me fail. That's why I disappeared.")
  - A pause. Then something shifts.
- **Duration:** 18 seconds
- **Dialogue:**
  - Marcus: "I did everything right. I got the grades, the job, the family. I'm still fighting just to stay above water. And she's here." *(gestures at the room, meaning: called here for you)*
  - Pure: "I know."
  - Marcus: "You don't."
  - Pure: "I know I'm the reason you think love is something you have to earn."
  - *(beat)*
  - Marcus: "...yeah."
- **Camera:** The door opening → Marcus at the foot of the bed → hold on both brothers in frame together — the first time they've been in the same shot in the film — → close on the exchange, face to face
- **Visual Notes:**
  - Their first shared shot in the film. The composition matters: equal weight, no one looming over the other, even though Marcus is standing and Pure is in the bed.
  - Delia remains visible in the background — her presence is what they're both orbiting, even in this conversation about each other
  - The dialogue is short by design — the whole history between them is understood, not explained
- **Audio Notes:** The quiet of the room beneath the dialogue. Nothing dramatic.
- **Next Beat:** 4.05 (the hug)
- **Failure Path:** None

---

#### Beat 4.05: THE HUG
- **Type:** Action (the film's emotional centerpiece — no dialogue)
- **Actor:** Marcus, Pure
- **Action:**
  - Marcus crosses to the side of the bed. Pure sits up slightly.
  - The hug is not graceful — Pure is still in a hospital bed, Marcus has to bend awkwardly — but it's real
  - Hold the composition. Don't cut. Let it sit.
- **Duration:** 15 seconds
- **Dialogue:** None
- **Camera:** Static medium — hold on the hug, unhurried; the camera does not move; this is not a crane shot or a circle-pan moment — it is simple, still, and heavy
- **Visual Notes:**
  - This deserves the same visual respect as a dramatic action beat in a more conventional film — just expressed as stillness and weight rather than movement
  - Delia in the background, watching. A single expression that carries twenty years of waiting for this.
  - The M2S register's "characters occupy space like the environment costs them something" applies here: the hug should feel like it has weight, like both of them are working to hold it
- **Audio Notes:** Nothing. Maybe the faintest ambient hospital sound. The silence is the sound design.
- **Next Beat:** 4.06 (home)
- **Failure Path:** None

## Scene 4C: The Deletion (1:01–1:46)

### Environment
- **Location:** Pure's home — same desk, same room as the collapse, but now in daylight (a deliberate change from the night collapse); the laptop is closed
- **Time:** Some days after discharge — the gap is implied, not staged

### Characters Present
- Pure (age 27 design, home clothes — simpler than his office/business wardrobe)

### Beat Sequence

#### Beat 4.06: HOME
- **Type:** Establishing
- **Actor:** Pure
- **Action:**
  - Pure at home, sitting. Not at his desk — in a different chair, the desk visible in background, the laptop on it, closed
  - He's just been sitting. Thinking. The room feels different without the glow of the running app.
- **Duration:** 8 seconds
- **Dialogue (V.O., Pure — quiet, internal):** "The memories I'd lost were already trying to come back. The shape of them. Outlines."
- **Camera:** Wide, resting — Pure in the chair, the desk in the background with the closed laptop
- **Visual Notes:** The closed laptop is a deliberate contrast to the glowing screen of the opening beat. But it's not deleted yet — he has to choose to delete it.
- **Audio Notes:** Day ambient — outdoor sounds, birds, ordinary world. The contrast to the institutional hospital and the late-night desk is intentional.
- **Next Beat:** 4.07 (the deletion)
- **Failure Path:** None

---

#### Beat 4.07: THE DELETION
- **Type:** Action
- **Actor:** Pure
- **Action:**
  - Pure sits at the desk, opens the laptop. The app is there.
  - He looks at it for one beat — not long, but long enough to feel the weight of what he built
  - Then he deletes it. Simple. Final. No dramatic hesitation.
- **Duration:** 12 seconds
- **Dialogue:** None during the action
- **Camera:** Over-shoulder to screen (app visible) → close on Pure's hand, cursor over the delete/uninstall → the click → screen clear
- **Visual Notes:**
  - The deletion should be filmed matter-of-factly, not with swelling music or a dramatic pause — the simplicity is the point. He's already made the decision. The deletion is just the act.
  - We don't need to see Earl's glitch or any visual confirmation that the app is "really" gone. The empty screen is enough.
- **Audio Notes:** The click of the delete confirmation. Then silence from the laptop (it's no longer running anything). That's it.
- **Next Beat:** 4.08 (the return)
- **Failure Path:** None

---

#### Beat 4.08: THE RETURN
- **Type:** Emotional (closing, mostly silent)
- **Actor:** Pure
- **Action:**
  - Pure sits back. A long, still beat.
  - Then: sound-design only — Delia's laugh. Somewhere in his memory, heard clearly for the first time in months. Then Marcus's voice, saying something mundane and brotherly. Then Earl — not the glitch, not the warning — something earlier, warmer.
  - Pure's face, not smiling exactly, but something behind the eyes reopening
- **Duration:** 15 seconds
- **Dialogue (V.O., adult Pure):** "The code that erased me turned out to be deletable too. The memories came back like something surfacing. Not all at once. But they came back."
- **Camera:** Close on Pure's face — hold; no cut until the V.O. ends and we dissolve to Sequence 5
- **Visual Notes:**
  - The returning memories are **audio-only** — no illustrated flashback images, no visual representation of the memories. This is a deliberate mirror of the memory-loss beats in Sequence 3 (3.07, 3.08), where what was absent was also shown only as absence. The mirror: what was taken as blankness, returned as sound.
  - Pure's face should carry the whole emotional weight of this beat — which is why this breakdown calls for a held close rather than a cut-away
- **Audio Notes:** Three brief audio fragments over the V.O., in sequence: Delia's laugh (warm, specific, not generic), a short phrase in Marcus's voice (indistinct but human), Earl's voice (young, from before — the same warm register as his Sequence 1 memory appearance, not the glitch register). These are sound-design assets that need to be sourced/recorded, not dialogue beats.
- **Next Beat:** (Sequence 5: The Courtroom)
- **Failure Path:** None

## Production Notes

### Scene List for Asset Creation
1. Home workspace — same environment as Sequence 3's opening/closing, now reused (night for the collapse, then day for the deletion)
2. Hospital room — new location; interior, diffuse light, minimal equipment shapes

### Character Designs Needed
- All from existing roster — no new designs
- Pure (age 27, contemporary) in two sub-states: hospital bed (passive, then reactive) and home clothes (deliberate, calm)
- Delia: age-locked generic; emotional register is different here than Sequence 1 — not tired-tender but something harder, more honest
- Marcus: age-locked generic; this is the most emotionally present we've seen him — the "entering, standing, bending to hug" animation states are new moments for this design

### Animation States
- **Pure:** lying still (hospital), turning to look, sitting up (for the hug), sitting at desk (deletion), sitting back (return)
- **Delia:** seated, hand on hand, speaking directly, watching in background
- **Marcus:** entering, standing at foot of bed, crossing room, bending to hug

### Key Audio Assets (not standard animation/dialogue)
- **Memory audio fragments** (Beat 4.08): three short sounds — Delia's laugh, Marcus's voice, Earl's warm (pre-glitch) voice. These are separate from any dialogue beat and need dedicated recording/sound-design. Not scripted as dialogue; described as character-specific audio memory.
- **Collapse sound design** (Beat 4.01): no crash, just a dragging key and then the hum of a running laptop in an empty room.

### Tone Notes
- This sequence is the film's pivot from deterioration to recovery — but it should not feel resolved yet. The hug lands. The deletion lands. But Sequence 5 immediately introduces a new obstacle (the denied expungement), so the emotional register here is: something cracking open, not something fixed.
- Delia's speech (Beat 4.03) is the sequence's non-negotiable. If any beat in the film gets extra time in post, it's this one. The delivery, the pauses, the held camera — everything should serve those three sentences.

### Key Emotional Beats (Non-Negotiable)
- ✅ The collapse at the desk, filmed matter-of-factly (not melodramatically)
- ✅ The hand-over-hand opening image in the hospital room
- ✅ Delia's speech filmed as a held close on her face — no cuts during the speech
- ✅ The brothers' first shared frame in the film
- ✅ The hug held static — no camera movement, no swelling score
- ✅ The deletion as a simple act, not a dramatic one
- ✅ The returning memories as audio-only, mirroring their absence in Sequence 3
