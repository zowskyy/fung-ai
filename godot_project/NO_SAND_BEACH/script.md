# NO SAND BEACH — Master Script

**Format:** Black-and-white hand-drawn animation short film
**Structure:** 7 sequences
**Status:** Full story locked. Beat-by-beat breakdown complete for Sequence 1 only; Sequences 2–7 need beat breakdowns authored before they can be converted to ScenePlan resources.

---

## THE SETUP

Pure grows up in Santa Ana, California — ten miles from the ocean, but he never touches the sand as a kid. Because the beach was for people who owned houses, not for kids whose mother worked three jobs just to keep the lights on.

His mother, Delia, is the backbone. She works as a home health aide, a hotel housekeeper, and a school cafeteria prep cook — back-to-back shifts, day after day. She comes home at 3 AM, kisses Pure on the forehead, drinks coffee, and goes back out at 6 AM. She never complains. Her knees ache. Her back is shot. But she never stops.

Pure has an older brother, Marcus, who did everything right — community college, stable job, family — and he's still struggling. He resents Pure because Pure got all their mother's attention, even though Pure was the one who kept messing up.

Their father, Earl, died when Pure was seven — shot in a drainage ditch with seventeen dollars in his pocket. He loved his family, but he loved the street more. That pattern haunts Pure his whole life.

## THE FALL

Pure gets arrested as a teenager. Then when he's twenty-one, he's framed for a crime he didn't commit and does ten years in prison. Inside, he teaches himself to code on a broken library computer. It's the first time in his life he feels in control. The code obeys him. The world doesn't.

When he gets out, he can't get a job anywhere. Every interview goes great — until the background check. Then the interviewer's face changes. Door after door slams shut.

---

## THE APP

So Pure builds an app. He calls it "No Sand Beach" — because he grew up seeing the ocean but never feeling the sand. The app rewrites his background. He gets the job. He gets the corner office overlooking Newport Beach. He's made it.

But the app has a dark side. It doesn't just erase his record — it starts erasing his actual memories. He forgets his mother's laugh. He forgets his brother's voice. He's becoming a ghost.

His father, Earl, starts appearing as a glitch on the screen. He says, "You built a lie and you're falling in love with it. You think you're honoring your mother by becoming someone else? You're killing her by becoming a stranger."

---

## THE COLLAPSE

Pure collapses. In the hospital, Delia holds his hand and says, "I worked three jobs because I didn't want you to think you had to be rich to be loved. But I made you think you had to be perfect to be worthy. I saved your body. I didn't save your heart. That's on you."

Marcus comes to the hospital. They finally have it out — the jealousy, the resentment, the love underneath. They hug for the first time in twenty years.

Pure goes home and deletes the app. His real memories slowly return.

---

## THE COURTROOM

Pure goes to court to get his record expunged the honest way. The judge denies him. Pure says, "The law doesn't respect me. But I'm not going to let that break me. Because my mother didn't break, so I won't either."

---

## THE SAND

Pure takes his whole family to a quiet stretch of Huntington Beach. He kneels down and touches the sand for the first time. Not as a symbol — just as sand.

He starts a free coding class for kids. One kid asks, "Pure, how did you make it?" Pure says, "I didn't make it. I came back. That's the only thing that matters."

Pure learned that love isn't a subscription — it's the sand under your feet, the ground you've been standing on your whole life. You just never looked down.

His mother didn't need him to be successful. She needed him to be present. His brother didn't need him to be perfect. He needed him to be real.

Love yourself. Love others. Anything can happen.
